## Additions by page

### 6. Logo overview

- Subtitle: Institutional mark standards
- Logo and tagline: Keep the logo separate from the tagline. The tagline remains an independent text treatment and must not be locked into the logo artwork.
- Use the approved logo artwork only. Keep clear space around the mark and preserve its proportions, color, and relationship to the page.

Page 6 is nearly full after the standalone seal section. These need a tighter layout or a move to page 7.

### 7. Logo clear space and minimum size

- Subtitle: Technical standards for protecting the official CCBC mark across print and digital applications.
- Conservative size note: When in doubt, use the larger reproduction size. Confirm seal legibility before release and use the best available official master file.

### 8. Logo misuse

- Intro: The CCBC logo is a protected identifier. Keep its proportions, relationship, and clarity intact across every application.

### 9. Core color palette

- Intro: CCBC uses a focused palette that balances institutional confidence, clear communication, and a warm civic welcome. Use these colors consistently across print and digital applications.
- Palette standards: No small Gold or Clear Sky text on White.

The master already has "Use only these seven colors. No extra colors and no gradients." and the 60/25/10/5 balance.

### 10. Color combinations and accessibility

- Subtitle: Preferred combinations for clarity, contrast, and reliable reproduction.
- Under "Prohibited pairings": Use clear labels, checkmarks, and prohibition markers so the distinction remains understandable without relying on color alone.

### 11. Typography

- Subtitle: Manrope + Inter
- Intro: A clear type system gives every CCBC message a confident voice. Use Manrope to lead attention and Inter to support reading, navigation, and everyday information.
- Hierarchy row: CTA · Manrope Bold · "Explore the guide"
- Optional cover text: Typography is more than letters. It shapes how we communicate, establishes hierarchy, and creates clarity across every touchpoint. Manrope brings character and presence. Inter delivers clarity and versatility. Together, they create balance, structure, and meaning.

### 12. Photography

- Subtitle: Real people. Real relationships. Real church life.
- Intro: Photography should make the life of Candelaria Conservative Baptist Church feel visible, welcoming, and true. Show real people, real relationships, and real church life. Aim for approximately 60% candid and 40% polished photography.
- The 60 / 40 balance: 60% Natural moments and active community life. 40% Intentional portraits and composed feature imagery.
- Polished: Use polished photography when a clear subject, composed frame, or confident presence supports the message.
  - Website headers — establish a focused visual welcome.
  - Major campaigns — carry a deliberate message across key touchpoints.
  - Leadership features — introduce people with clarity and warmth.
- Candid: Use candid photography to show participation, connection, and everyday ministry in motion.
  - Worship — shared attention, singing, prayer, and welcome.
  - Fellowship — conversation, laughter, meals, and belonging.
  - Service — people contributing with care and purpose.
  - Discipleship — learning, mentoring, and growing together.
  - Everyday ministry — the ordinary moments that build church life.
- Editorial framing: Use open compositions, visible context, and generous negative space. Let the subject feel present in a real place rather than isolated from community.
- Crop cue: keep gestures, relationships, and environmental context visible.
- Crop cue: give portraits a calm, intentional focal point.
- Reflect the actual people and community of Candelaria. Favor natural expressions, warm light, environmental context, and natural skin tones. Avoid generic stock worship scenes, exaggerated emotion, heavy filters, artificial lens effects, and staged ‘perfect family’ images.
- Permission: Obtain appropriate permission before prominently featuring children or identifiable people.

The current page 12 is filled by three photographs. This text needs a second photography page, which makes the guide 19 pages.

### 13. Graphic elements and layout

- Subtitle: A simple system for clear movement and growth
- Spacing table: 8 pt, small separation within a related group. 16 pt, space between supporting elements. 24 pt, space between content blocks. 32 pt, major section transition.
- Pattern table:
  - Statement: blue field, left-aligned headline, one dominant message. Use a strong field and generous negative space to make one idea unmistakable.
  - Standards: rising line, aligned columns, restrained Gold marker. Use a measured grid to present rules, principles, or repeatable guidance.
  - Application: expanding block, information group, clear response. Use connected blocks to move the reader from context to action.
- Layout check: Keep one dominant message, align every element to the six-column grid, and preserve generous negative space. Use White and Warm Cream as breathing room; reserve Gold for focused accents.

### 14. Voice and bilingual writing

- Subtitle: Warm, clear, encouraging, biblical, and Christ-centered
- Intro: Voice is the consistent way CCBC sounds across announcements, invitations, teaching, care, and community updates. Keep one dominant message, make the next step clear, and leave room for people to respond with hope.
- Voice principles:
  - Personal — Address people directly and make the invitation feel human.
  - Clear — Use familiar words, short sentences, and one clear next step.
  - Hopeful — Invite with grace rather than guilt, pressure, or hype.
  - Biblical — Keep the message grounded in Christ without relying on unexplained church jargon.
- Focused bilingual callout: Let the audience, setting, and pastoral purpose guide the language choice. A natural blend should feel welcoming and easy to understand.

### 15. Bulletin application

- Subtitle: Clear hierarchy for Sunday communication
- Intro: Two editable bulletin cover directions for Sunday communication: one calm and spacious, one economical and low-ink.
- One message, two practical expressions: Both versions keep the cover hierarchy clear, place practical details together, and protect generous white space around the central message.
- Editorial note: Use a clear cover hierarchy, comfortable type, and a predictable order. Keep practical details together, leave enough white space, and retain a grayscale-safe low-ink version for economical reproduction.
- Application cues:
  - Calm version — Warm Cream, Deep Navy, CCBC Blue, and restrained Gold/Clear Sky accents.
  - Low-ink version — White, Charcoal, and Deep Navy with minimal ink coverage.
  - Shared system — Strong left alignment, restrained bands, rising lines, and expanding blocks.

The current page 15 is filled by the two bulletin mockups and has its own title, "A welcome worth keeping." This text needs the mockups made smaller, or a second page.

### 16. Website and newsletter applications

- Subtitle: Spacious, story-led, easy to scan
- Intro: Digital touchpoints carry the same clear, welcoming system across responsive web and editorial email formats.
- Core expression — “Going for Christ, Growing in Christ” should remain easy to find, easy to scan, and supported by one dominant message.
- Use strong left alignment, generous negative space, restrained angled bands, rising lines, and expanding blocks within the six-column grid.
- Application guidance:
  - Website — lead with one message, one primary “Learn more” action, and protected crop areas around live text.
  - Newsletter — open with a story-led hierarchy, descriptive headings, short sections, and generous scanning space.
  - Use the six-column grid, 4 mm gutters, 8 pt rhythm, 15 mm outer safe margins, and 18 mm binding-side margin.
  - Use Manrope for headings and Inter for body copy across both applications.

### 17. Event poster application

- Subtitle: One message, one clear next step
- Intro: Build every event poster around one dominant message, generous negative space, and a clear path from headline to grouped details. Use restrained angled bands, rising lines, and expanding blocks within the six-column grid.
- Application note: Keep one dominant message: let “Community Fellowship” lead, use the rising Clear Sky line to guide the eye, and keep “Date · Time · Location” with “Learn more” as one grouped details block.
- Details: Group practical information together so the poster remains easy to scan at a glance.
- Hierarchy: Lead with the event name, keep the visual field bold and quiet, and reserve the Gold accent for one focused point of attention.

The master's Hierarchy and Details block paragraphs are worded differently. The source wording would replace them.

### 18. Production standards and pre-publication checklist

- Subtitle: Ready for review, print, and publication
- Intro: A calm final check for consistent, accessible, production-ready CCBC communications.
- Under "Production standards": Use these standards across print, digital, church-office, and professional production workflows.
- Production flow: Prepare, Check, Export, Archive.
  - 01 Prepare: Approved assets and source files
  - 02 Check: Content, contrast, permissions
  - 03 Export: Correct preset and final size
  - 04 Archive: Editable source and approved export
- Under the checklist heading: Confirm each item before publishing, printing, or distributing.
- Heading wording: the source says "Pre-publication checklist"; the master says "Before publishing".
- Sign-off: the source has three fields, Reviewer, Date, and Approved for release; the master has two.
